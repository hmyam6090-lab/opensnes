# Temmie battle

An Undertale-style encounter with Temmie for the SNES, written in C on OpenSNES.

```bash
make            # builds my-game.sfc
make test       # runs the luna manifests in test/
```

## Controls

| Where | Keys |
|---|---|
| Menu | LEFT/RIGHT to move, A to confirm |
| Sub-menus | UP/DOWN (ACT), LEFT/RIGHT (Yes/No), A to confirm, B to go back |
| Text | A skips the typing, then A again continues |
| Dodging | D-pad moves the SOUL |
| End screen | START plays again |
| Debug (`GAME_DEBUG`) | in the menu: SELECT toggles Temmie Flakes, L cycles the forced attack (RANDOM / TALL / ANNOYED / ARM), R drops HP to 1; during an attack: START ends it |

## Layout

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `main.c` | boot and the main loop |
| `src/battle.c` | the state machine: menu, ACT, MERCY, narration, speech, dodge, end |
| `src/attacks.c` | attack A (tall Temmie), its annoyed variant, attack B (the arm) |
| `src/content.c` | **every line of text and every attack tunable** |
| `src/config.h` | build switches, player numbers, screen layout |
| `src/textbox.c` | typewriter text with word wrap |
| `src/platform.c` | the only file that drives video and input through the SDK |
| `src/sound.c` | sound effects (audio module) and the music hook |
| `src/sprite_layout.c/.h` | generated by `tools/build_sprites.py`: every image as a metasprite |

## The attacks

- **Tall Temmie (A).** The `tall` image (head and body) stands on four
  wobbly legs. She walks left and right on her own, trying to make a leg
  catch you. Each walk goes to a random spot at a random speed, with a
  pause in between, and some walks are feints that turn around half way.
  You stay safe by following her inside the cage between her inner legs.
  Her fastest walk (`speed_max`) is a little slower than the SOUL, so you
  can keep up. Body and legs hurt, and her head doesn't. She stays inside
  the board. If you stand still for the whole attack, you lose about
  18 HP.
- **Annoyed (A').** This comes after you refuse to give her the Flakes.
  Temmie looks smug, and "can't blame a BARK for tryin'..." shows as the
  picture. The SOUL starts on the floor in front of her, and the safe spot
  is on top of her back. She walks faster, pauses less and feints more
  often.
- **The arm (B).** Her arm comes down from her portrait into a 12x12
  board. It moves one cell at a time on the board's 8 px grid, like
  Snake. The arm is a 4 px line drawn with background tiles, so it lines up
  exactly with the grid. The hand (`temmie_attack_4`) glides from cell to
  cell. At every cell it turns toward the SOUL, and it never reverses or
  crosses itself. The hand and the line both hurt. It pulls back when it
  reaches `max_cells`, when it's boxed in, or when the SOUL traces the
  edges and stops near the middle of one.

Every number above is in `content.c` (`tall_params`, `arm_params`,
`tall_boxes`, `tall_leg_x`, `atk_box`).

## Death

At 0 HP everything disappears except the SOUL. It stays frozen, then
cracks (`soul_broken`, with the `SFX_HEART_CRACK` sound), then shatters
into shards that fly up and fall (`SFX_HEART_SHATTER`), and then GAME OVER
appears. The timings and the gravity are the `DEATH_*` values in
`config.h`. The broken heart and the shards are drawn in
`tools/build_sprites.py`, because the art in `assets/` doesn't include
them.

## Defaults for the other open questions

- **Feed with Flakes.** You get a Yes/No prompt. Yes plays "FOOB!!!", then
  "OMG!! humans TOO CUTE", and you win. No starts the annoyed attack.
  Without Flakes, Temmie says "NO!!! so hungr..." and the fight ends with
  her leaving.
- **Talk.** One Talk makes her sparable. Temmie answers with a random line
  from the neutral pool.
- **Attack order.** Each turn picks randomly between TALL and ARM. The
  annoyed variant only comes after the refusal.

The three outcome lines in `content.c` (`out_spared`, `out_fed`,
`out_left`) are placeholder wording and are not from the source text.

## Replacing assets

- **Art.** All the art is in `assets/`. After changing it, run:

  ```bash
  python3 tools/build_sprites.py
  ```

  The script packs the art into `res/sprites.png` and regenerates
  `src/sprite_layout.c/.h`. Each image is cut into 16x16 pieces, empty
  pieces are dropped and identical ones are shared, and it stops if the
  sheet goes over the 512 sprite tiles of VRAM. The current sheet uses 232.
  `make` then converts the sheet with `opensnes-sprite`, so the build
  itself never runs Python. The width of tall Temmie's stretched body is
  `TALL_STRETCH` in the script.
- **Art that's in `assets/` but not used.** `temmie_hurt.png` would only
  appear when you FIGHT, and FIGHT isn't implemented. The Japanese smug
  text isn't used either. The `*_4x.png` files are previews.
- **Sound.** See the next section.

## Sound

Only one sound backend runs at a time, because each one uploads its own
program to the SNES sound chip. You choose it with `GAME_SOUND` in
`src/config.h`.

- **`SOUND_GSS` (default): SNESGSS.** This plays the music and any sound
  effects made in the same SNESGSS project. The battle music is
  `res/gss/music_1.bin`.
- **`SOUND_SAMPLES`: the SDK's audio module.** This plays the WAV sound
  effects in `res/sfx_*.wav`, but no music.

The game plays a sound hook at every moment that could make a sound. A
hook with nothing assigned stays silent.

| Hook | When it plays |
|---|---|
| `SFX_MOVE` | a menu cursor moves |
| `SFX_SELECT` | a menu choice is confirmed |
| `SFX_BACK` | B leaves a sub-menu |
| `SFX_DENIED` | MERCY is pressed before Temmie is sparable |
| `SFX_BLIP` | narration is typing |
| `SFX_TEMMIE_VOICE` | Temmie's speech is typing |
| `SFX_SMUG` | the "can't blame a BARK" picture appears |
| `SFX_ATTACK_START` | an attack begins |
| `SFX_TEMMIE_STEP` | a foot of tall Temmie lands while she walks |
| `SFX_ARM_STEP` | the arm moves one cell |
| `SFX_ARM_RETRACT` | the arm starts pulling back |
| `SFX_HURT` | the SOUL takes damage |
| `SFX_HEART_CRACK` | death: the SOUL cracks |
| `SFX_HEART_SHATTER` | death: the SOUL shatters |
| `SFX_SPARE` | Temmie is spared |
| `SFX_EAT` | Temmie eats the Flakes |
| `SFX_WIN` | you win by feeding her, or she runs off |
| `BGM_BATTLE` | music: the fight starts |
| `BGM_WIN`, `BGM_LOSE` | music: you win, GAME OVER |
| `BGM_NONE` | music: stops when you die |

### SNESGSS (`SOUND_GSS`)

From each SNESGSS export you only need `spc700.bin` (the sound driver) and
the `music_N.bin` files. `gss_data.asm` replaces the exported
`sounds.asm`, and the exported `sounds.h` isn't used.

- **Update the music:** export again from SNESGSS, copy `spc700.bin` and
  `music_1.bin` over the ones in `res/gss/`, then run `make`.
- **Looping is done in code,** so you don't need a loop point in
  SNESGSS. When the game loads a song, `sound.c` finds each channel's
  end jump (`FF` followed by an address, which an unlooped song points at
  its own last byte) and patches it to jump back to that channel's start.
  `gss_music_loop` in `src/sound.c` sets which songs loop. A song that
  already has a loop point (Ctrl+Home in SNESGSS) keeps it.
- **Add another song,** for example a win theme:
  1. Copy `music_2.bin` into `res/gss/`.
  2. In `gss_data.asm`, add an `ASSET_SECTION` block that includes it,
     with the label `gss_music_2`.
  3. In `src/sound.c`, add `extern const u8 gss_music_2[];`.
  4. In the `gss_music` table in `src/sound.c`, set
     `[BGM_WIN] = gss_music_2,`.

  A song hook set to `0` stops the music.
- **Sound effects play together with the music.** Both run on the
  SNESGSS driver, because only one program can drive the SNES sound chip.
  You don't need to make the effects in SNESGSS: the WAV files in
  `assets/sfx/` are added to the driver by `tools/gss_add_sfx.py`. That
  script resamples each WAV, encodes it as BRR, and adds it to the driver
  as an instrument plus a one-note effect. It writes
  `res/gss/spc700_sfx.bin` (the driver the game loads), the sample data
  (`res/gss/sfx_bank_N.bin` and `gss_sfx_data.asm`), and `src/gss_sfx.h`.
  Your song stays at the address SNESGSS gave it.
  - **To change a sound:** replace its WAV in `assets/sfx/`, then run
    `python3 tools/gss_add_sfx.py` and `make`.
  - **To add one:** put the WAV in `assets/sfx/`, add an `[[sfx]]` entry
    to `res/gss/sfx.toml`, and run the script. Then give the hook its
    effect in the `gss_sfx` table in `src/sound.c`, for example
    `[SFX_SMUG] = { GSS_SFX_SMUG, 110, 5 },`. The three numbers are the
    effect, the volume (0–127) and the channel.
  - **After a new SNESGSS export:** copy `spc700.bin` and `music_1.bin`
    in as before, then run the script again.
  - **Channels:** the song uses channel 0. An effect only plays on a
    channel the song doesn't use, and each channel plays one effect at a
    time. Text plays on channel 7, menus on 6, and hurt and death sounds
    on 5. A song that used channels 5 to 7 would silence those effects.
  - **Space:** the six effects use about 33 KB of the 64 KB of sound
    RAM, at 31.5 kHz (`note = 71` in `sfx.toml`). The script reports what
    is left. `note = 60` halves the size at 16.7 kHz.
  - **Boot:** uploading the effects takes about a second at power-on.
    The screen stays black during it.

After `INITIALIZE`, the game sends `STEREO` (on). Without it the driver
runs in mono and plays every voice at full volume on both sides, twice as
loud as SNESGSS's preview and its `.spc` export. Measured on the same
emulator with stereo on, the game matches the `.spc`: same loudness, same
pitch, envelope correlation 0.99.

The code that loads and talks to the driver follows SNESGSS's own
`snes/sneslib.asm` and `sneslib.h` from the
[KungFuFurby/snesgss](https://github.com/KungFuFurby/snesgss) copy of
Shiru's code. The driver is uploaded at `$0200` and sent `INITIALIZE`. To
play a song, the game sends `LOAD`, uploads the song at the address in
bytes 14–15 of `spc700.bin`, restarts the driver at `$0200`, and sends
`MUSIC_PLAY`. The uploads use the SDK's `apu` module. If the driver
doesn't answer, the game goes silent instead of freezing.

### WAV sound effects (`SOUND_SAMPLES`)

1. Put the sound in `res/` as a WAV file: mono, 16-bit PCM, a short
   one-shot. For example, `res/sfx_step.wav`. Next to it, add a copy of
   any `res/sfx_*.wav.toml` named `sfx_step.wav.toml`. `make` converts the
   WAV and writes `res/sfx_step.h`.
2. In `src/sound.c`, add `#include "../res/sfx_step.h"` next to the other
   includes.
3. Fill the hook's row in `sfx_table`: `[SFX_TEMMIE_STEP] = SFX(sfx_step, 90),`.

To replace a sound, overwrite its WAV file and rebuild. The driver holds
up to 64 samples, in about 58 KB of sound RAM. To regenerate the
placeholder beeps, run `python3 tools/make_placeholder_sfx.py [names]`.

**To add a new hook (either backend):** add an id in `src/sound.h`, call
`sound_sfx(SFX_YOUR_ID);` where the event happens, then give it a sound
in the backend's table.

## Not implemented

- FIGHT shows a "not implemented" line.
- ITEM only reports whether you hold the Flakes.
- The game stalls for 1 to 2 frames when a box is redrawn between states.
  Attacks run with no lag frames. Sprites go through the SDK's assembly
  `oamDrawMetasprite`, Temmie's portrait sits in fixed OAM slots, and the
  legs and the arm are each drawn in one call.
